## Ownership and resilience

- `TimelineRail.tsx`: ordered real records; no text is hidden behind interaction or animation.
- `useActiveTimelineRecord.ts`: one reading focus from passive native scroll; no imperative scroll selection or hover owner.
- `TimelineStage.tsx`: near-viewport dynamic import, persisted effect mode, and React lifetime boundary.
- `timelineModels.ts`: original procedural record-specific geometry, category fallbacks, shared materials, operation poses and exact moving contact points.
- `timelineCompanion.ts`: the volumetric bird, feather layers and wing/head/foot articulation.
- `timelineActivity.ts`: the shared reach/manipulate/release envelope for bird and artifact.
- `timelineRenderer.ts`: viewport sizing, projection, bounded animation scheduling, context loss/restoration, and idempotent cleanup including partial initialization and frame failures.
- The global archive renderer no longer owns any Timeline paths, nodes, buffers or uniforms. Timeline retains its section index so neighboring scenes keep their original transitions.

FULL continuously damps toward the active pose and repeats the active artifact’s manipulation after landing. Each inactive artifact retains its last operation phase and damps its operation weight down, so reversing mid-action does not snap a page or key back. STATIC invalidates once on reading/resize/mode or deliberate bird interaction. Hidden and offscreen scenes cancel RAF; unmount or terminal render failure releases geometry, materials, renderer and observers/listeners. Context restoration remeasures and redraws. A one-pixel Canvas 2D swatch only converts CSS colors; it is not a visual renderer, and no textures or external assets are loaded. The backing store is capped at DPR 1.5 and 2048 pixels per dimension.

### Flight dynamics, grooming and density pass

A user review identified the core remaining stiffness: the bird translated with folded wings. Two mechanisms caused it — the position damper asymptotically glides the last stretch after the docking flag settles, and following a moving work contact (turning page, sliding cursor) drags the body sideways with no wing response. Flight is now velocity-driven: measured body speed raises the flight weight even when the docking state reads stationary, capped to a light flutter while the beak is holding a task surface, so any visible translation carries visible wing use. The body itself answers the wingbeat — it lifts on the downstroke and rocks around its length, pitch follows vertical velocity, the damping rate rises slightly mid-flight, and the slow sinusoidal hover drift was replaced by the flap-synced motion. Takeoff adds a push-off dip with a tail snap; landing dips deeper with a wing flare.

Preening joined the behavior set: on an incommensurate 23.7/31.3-second rhythm the bird pauses its work cycle (the renderer drops the work weight while the preen weight is high), turns its head back over the shoulder and nibbles at the folded wing with a pulsing gape, then resumes. The renderer exposes the damped preen weight as `companionPreen` for diagnostics. Idle glance and gaze amplitudes were raised, and a small auricular tract plus higher tessellation (body 54×30, neck 46×26, feathers 21×11, roughly doubling vertex density) answer the request for a denser model.

Headless verification caught an environment trap worth recording: software rendering ran at ~7fps, and because the animation clock caps per-frame delta, measured velocities inflated ~3.5×, making the bird flap constantly in that environment only. At device scale 1 (~13fps) velocity reads clean: work-cycle flights sit near 0.01–0.28 with 0.0000 contact error, and a real preen window was captured (flight 0.01, beak released from the artifact). Summon flight frames show distinct downstroke/recovery wing poses. Console stayed clean; typecheck, Timeline ESLint and the production build passed.

### Convulsion fix: debounced flight transitions and stabilized wing control

A user review reported the bird convulsing — repeated up-down jerks after interacting at an artifact and constant twitching in the air. The mechanism was introduced with velocity-driven wings: raw per-frame speed noise fed the flight weight, so its value chatter-crossed the bare .2/.25 thresholds, and every crossing re-fired the landing and takeoff body envelopes — a visible convulsion. Three layered fixes: (1) takeoff/landing now require debounced, decisive transitions using a flight peak tracked since the last ground contact; (2) the wing controller runs on a damped speed estimate, the airborne state latches with hysteresis (open past .15 distance, close below .06), the flight term left the position damping rate, and the pitch response gained a dead band — while the beak holds a task the wings stay folded except for a genuine hop (speed above 1.2 units/s); (3) the wingbeat slowed from 5.4 to 4.3 Hz with a waxing depth modulation so sustained hover reads as wingbeats rather than vibration, and the body coupling shrank accordingly.

Programmatic verification samples the live diagnostics at display frame rate via requestAnimationFrame. Hovering over a record gap: flight pinned at 1.00 across 120 frames, zero threshold crossings, zero screen-direction reversals. Working at an artifact across 300 frames (~1.2 cycles): six crossings and three reversals, matching the single choreographed perch-to-contact hop per cycle rather than chatter. Contact error stayed 0.0000 while demonstrating and the console stayed clean. Typecheck, Timeline ESLint and the production build passed.
